# FunnelIQ — Findings & Recommendations

Business write-up for Northbound Media's founder. One section per work package; every number is
taken from `models/metrics.json` or `docs/FINDINGS.md` (both generated by code), never typed by hand.
P1 lives in [`FINDINGS.md`](FINDINGS.md).

## P2 — How long will a new customer stay? (regression on `ltv_months`)

**Setup.** Customers only (purchased = 1, n = 3,163; 4 rows with a missing lifetime dropped — decisions
D-M2-1 / D-M2-2). Features: the 14 acquisition-time funnel fields plus four derived ratios
(answer rate, conversion rate, cost per lead, budget tier). Three gradient-boosting regressors,
5-fold cross-validation, seed 42.

| model | RMSE (months) | R² |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| XGBoost | 2.84 ± 0.17 | 0.944 |
| LightGBM | 2.86 ± 0.16 | 0.943 |
| **CatBoost (served)** | **2.80 ± 0.16** | **0.946** |
| ensemble (mean of three) | 2.81 ± 0.17 | 0.945 |

Lifetime in the data has a standard deviation of 12.0 months; the model's error is ±2.8 months.

### Should `cumulative_profit` be a feature? No.

Profit accrues *over* the lifetime we are predicting: a customer who stays 40 months has had 40 months
to generate profit. Using it would mean predicting the future with the future. The ablation makes the
leak visible: on the same rows, CatBoost goes from R² 0.946 (funnel only) to **0.975** when
`cumulative_profit` is added. That "improvement" is exactly the leakage, and it is why the feature policy in
`ml/features.py` rejects every outcome column and the leakage guard is a unit test, not a comment.

### Which features dominate, and do the three models agree?

Yes, unusually strongly. With gain-based importances on all three:

| feature | XGBoost | LightGBM | CatBoost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `calls_to_closed` | 0.86 | 0.83 | 0.92 |
| `ad_budget` | 0.09 | 0.14 | 0.02 |
| everything else | 0.05 | 0.03 | 0.06 |

The reason is in the raw data: average lifetime is a staircase on calls-to-close — 36 months when the deal
closed in 1–2 calls, 28 at 3, 18 at 4, 12 at 5, and about 7 at 6 or more, with only ±3 months of spread
inside each step (correlation −0.95). Budget tier looks important on its own (Mid 33.5 months vs High 13.2
vs Low 8.9) but mostly acts *through* calls-to-close: Mid-tier campaigns bring customers who close in 1–4
calls, Low and High bring customers who need 3–9. At equal calls-to-close the tiers differ by only 2–5 months.

### The strongest lever on customer longevity, in two sentences

Customers who are easy to close stay; customers who needed five or more calls leave within a year. Northbound
should treat calls-to-close as an early-warning signal at the moment of signing — route hard-won customers to
onboarding and retention effort immediately, and steer acquisition toward the Mid-budget campaigns whose
leads close fast, rather than paying more per customer in the High tier for shorter relationships.

**Caveat.** An R² of 0.95 with steps this clean is unusual; on real agency data the same relationship exists
but is noisier. The method (feature policy, cross-validation, leakage ablation, explaining what the model
learned) is what transfers.
